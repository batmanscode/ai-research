Exploratory finding · FLIP2 Hydro

Aggregate chemistry transfers.
Across three protein backbones.

Can a model learn from mutations in some proteins and still rank variants in a protein it did not train on? Here, a small model with 11–16 position-free chemistry features beat mutation count alone on all three held-out backbones.

Important: earlier study choices were influenced by the public test data. These are descriptive results, not untouched confirmation.

The problem

Protein positions do not line up neatly.

The seven tested amino-acid positions sit in different structural contexts in these three proteins. Calling each one “site 1” or “site 7” makes the positions look comparable when they are not. The question was whether a small summary of overall chemical change could travel better.

The correction

`VVVVVVV` was not the native sequence.

The first analysis counted non-valine residues. Canonical mapping showed that the all-valine sequence is an engineered anchor. The analysis was retracted and rebuilt with the native cores.

Why it may transfer

Keep the chemistry. Drop the position labels.

The seven randomized sites are buried, but “site 1” in one protein does not match “site 1” in another. The model summarizes chemical changes without pretending those positions line up.

7 core residues→signed + absolute property changes→11–16 invariant features→ridge ranking
Useful failure: adding naive position-specific 3D features reduced held-backbone performance in all three folds.

The evidence boundary

A fourth backbone is the decisive experiment.

  1. 01Freeze the features, model settings, and metrics before seeing labels.
  2. 02Assay 1,000–2,000 variants that pass quality checks, spread across mutation counts.
  3. 03Prepare at least three independent replicate libraries.
  4. 04Report the result whether it confirms or falsifies transfer.
