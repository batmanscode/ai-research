In plain English

A guard must be able to move without exposing anyone.

1

Cover

Every unguarded point must touch at least one guard. That is ordinary domination.

2

Respond

If any unguarded point is attacked, an adjacent guard moves there.

3

Stay covered

After the move, every point must still touch a guard. That extra condition is security.

How it was found

The failed ideas mattered.

  1. 01

    Small graphs agreed

    Every eligible graph through order 10 obeyed γs ≤ α, so the stronger statement looked plausible.

  2. 02

    False shortcuts broke

    Maximum independent sets, dominating paths, and a tempting dominating-edge lemma all failed as general proof routes.

  3. 03

    SAT targeted the residue

    The search encoded α = 3, P₅-freeness, connectivity, and failure of every triple—without enumerating every graph.

  4. 04

    Symmetry revealed the object

    The 12-vertex model was recognized as the complement of an icosahedron, then independently verified without the SAT clauses.

What remains open

The answer lives somewhere between 4/3 and 3/2.

The counterexample rules out coefficient 1. It does not show whether the published 3/2 bound is optimal. Finding an α = 4 graph with γs = 6 would settle optimality; searches found none through order 14.
